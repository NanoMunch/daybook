Daybook

A private, day-by-day notebook that lives only on this iPhone.

Add it to your home screen

  1. Make sure this page is open in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share button. On newer iPhones it’s inside the ••• menu at the bottom right.
  3. Tap Add to Home Screen. You may need to tap View More to see it. If you see Open as Web App, leave it on.
  4. Tap Add, then open Daybook from your home screen.

From then on, always use the home-screen icon. Notes written there stay in that app, separate from Safari, and nothing is sent anywhere.

      Privacy

      Everything you write is stored in this app on this iPhone and nowhere else. Daybook is locked so it can’t send anything over the internet. The only thing it downloads is new versions of its own code.

      Search and On this day work on this iPhone too. What you search for isn’t saved or sent anywhere.

      To-dos, pins and #tags are worked out from your own text on this iPhone, like everything else.

      Collections (Links, Money, Addresses and the rest) are sorted on this iPhone by simple rules that look at your text. Nothing is sent anywhere to sort them. A link, number or address only goes anywhere if you tap it: links open in Safari, numbers in Phone, addresses in Apple Maps.

      Coming up (the calendar button) finds days and times in your notes on this iPhone with simple rules. Nothing is added to your Calendar or sent anywhere.

      Photos are re-saved on your iPhone without their hidden details, such as where they were taken. Photos you take with Daybook’s camera normally aren’t added to your Photos app. To keep a copy there, open the photo and tap Save.

      Voice notes are recorded and kept only on this iPhone. Daybook uses the microphone only while the recording panel is open, and the iPhone shows its orange dot while it does.

      Writing tips

      • Tap the tick box button next to the note box, or start a line with [ ], to make a to-do. Return starts the next one; Return on an empty one ends the list.
      • Tick a box right on the page. No need to open the note.
      • Open a note and tap the pin to keep it at the top of that day.
      • With the note box empty, tap the microphone to record a voice note. Tap the note’s words to add a caption, which search can find.
      • Rather have it written? Tap the microphone on the iPhone keyboard to dictate into the note box. iPhones with on-device dictation do this without sending your voice anywhere.
      • Write a day or time in a note, like dentist Fri 3pm or Mum’s birthday 12 Dec, and it appears in Coming up (the calendar button) and at the top of that day’s page.
      • Write a word with # in front, like #garden, and it becomes a tag. Tap a tag to see every note with it.

      Back up your notes

      Choose a password for this backup. You’ll need it to restore. It isn’t saved anywhere, so if you forget it, the backup can’t be opened by anyone, including you.

      Encrypting…

      Tap Save, then choose Save to Files and pick a place you trust, such as On My iPhone or iCloud Drive.

      Backup saved.

      Restore from a backup

      Pick a .daybook backup file, then enter the password you chose when you made it. Nothing changes until you confirm.
